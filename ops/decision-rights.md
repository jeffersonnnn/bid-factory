# Decision rights

Complete the open values before the operator starts paid work.

## The operator can decide alone

- Implement and test code inside the agreed product scope.
- Fix defects that preserve the documented product rules.
- Improve internal documentation and synthetic fixtures.
- Run local tests and synthetic model calls within the approved budget.
- Research public opportunities, contractors, and reviewers.
- Prepare draft scans, outreach, orders, invoices, and reviewer requests.
- Stop work when a documented blocker exists.

## Jefferson must approve first

- Change the pilot price, deposit, scope, refund terms, or delivery promise.
- Spend more than $[amount] on one item.
- Spend more than $[amount] in one month.
- Hire or pay a reviewer.
- Send customer outreach or start a Monid campaign.
- Accept a customer order or invoice a customer.
- Upload customer documents to a new provider.
- Deploy the app to a public or remote host.
- Grant repository, payment, domain, or customer-folder access.
- Change data retention, deletion, security, or model-provider terms.
- Release final customer files.

## The customer must decide

- Whether to pursue the RFQ.
- Whether its company meets eligibility and performance conditions.
- Which facts, references, staff, and commitments are accurate.
- All prices and pricing assumptions.
- Which person signs the required forms.
- Whether to accept the review PDF.
- When and how to submit the final package.

## The reviewer must decide

- Whether the requirement list missed an obligation.
- Whether the package follows the supplied instructions and evaluation factors.
- Whether a finding is closed in the exact corrected version.
- Whether to accept, accept after corrections, or reject the package.

## No one may override

- A missing mandatory requirement.
- A missing customer price approval.
- An unprocessed amendment.
- An expired deadline or missed mandatory site visit.
- An unsupported factual claim.
- An unresolved reviewer finding.
- A failed exact-version check.
